Islamic Studies

Beliefs, practice, and sources

A short course in the fundamentals: what Muslims believe, what they practice daily, and where it all comes from.

Practice

The Five Pillars of Islam

These five acts form the framework of Muslim life — belief and worship expressed as daily, yearly, and lifetime practice.

Shahada — the declaration of faith

Bearing witness that there is no god but God, and that Muhammad ﷺ is His messenger. This declaration is the entry point into Islam and is repeated throughout daily prayer.

Salah — the five daily prayers

Ritual prayer performed five times a day — at dawn, midday, afternoon, sunset, and night — facing the Kaaba in Mecca. It structures the Muslim day around remembrance of God.

Zakat — almsgiving

A fixed portion of savings, typically 2.5% annually, given to those in need. It is treated as a right of the poor rather than an optional act of charity.

Sawm — fasting in Ramadan

Abstaining from food, drink, and other needs from dawn to sunset throughout the month of Ramadan, practiced as an act of discipline, gratitude, and solidarity with the poor.

Hajj — pilgrimage to Mecca

A pilgrimage to Mecca performed once in a lifetime by every Muslim who is physically and financially able, held during a specific set of days in the Islamic calendar.

Sources

The Qur'an and the Hadith

The Qur'an is held by Muslims to be the literal word of God, revealed to Muhammad ﷺ over 23 years and preserved unchanged since. It is organized into 114 chapters (surahs) and is recited in its original Arabic in worship worldwide.

The Hadith are collected records of what Muhammad ﷺ said, did, or approved of. Alongside the Qur'an, they form the Sunnah — the second major source Muslims draw on for guidance in daily life, law, and worship.

Belief

Six Articles of Faith

Islamic belief (Aqeedah) rests on six pillars: belief in the oneness of God (Tawhid), His angels, His revealed books, His prophets, the Day of Judgment, and divine decree — that God's will encompasses all that happens.

Character

Akhlaq — Islamic Ethics

Islam places heavy emphasis on personal character: honesty, humility, patience, generosity, and justice — even toward one's enemies. Muhammad ﷺ is described in the Qur'an as possessing "an exalted standard of character," and his life is treated as the model Muslims strive to follow.

Local history

Islam among the Yorùbá

Islam reached Yorùbá land centuries ago through trans-Saharan trade routes connecting West Africa to North Africa and the wider Muslim world, well before European colonization. Over generations it became deeply woven into Yorùbá life, producing a long tradition of Yorùbá-speaking scholars, Qur'anic schools, and a distinct local Islamic culture that continues today.

This is a brief overview, not a full academic history — a good next step for a deeper dive is speaking with a local imam or Islamic studies teacher.
