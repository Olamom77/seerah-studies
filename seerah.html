Seerah — the life story

The Life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Ten moments from a life that begins in Mecca in 570 CE and reshapes the history of Arabia and beyond. Press the speaker to have this timeline read aloud.

  1. 570 CE · Mecca

    Birth and Early Life

    Muhammad ﷺ was born into the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe. His father Abdullah died before he was born, and his mother Aminah died when he was six. He was raised first by his grandfather Abd al-Muttalib, then by his uncle Abu Talib. As a young man he worked in trade and became known across Mecca as Al-Amin — "the Trustworthy."

  2. 595 CE · Mecca

    Marriage to Khadijah

    At 25, Muhammad ﷺ married Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, a respected and successful businesswoman fifteen years his senior, after managing a trading caravan for her. Their marriage was one of deep partnership; Khadijah was the first person to believe in his prophethood and remained his closest support until her death.

  3. 610 CE · Cave of Hira

    The First Revelation

    At 40, during a retreat in the Cave of Hira outside Mecca, Muhammad ﷺ received the first verses of the Qur'an through the Angel Jibril (Gabriel). The angel's command was a single word: "Iqra" — "Read." This moment marked the beginning of twenty-three years of revelation.

  4. 613–619 CE · Mecca

    Public Preaching and Persecution

    After three years of quiet teaching, Muhammad ﷺ began preaching openly, calling people to the worship of one God and away from idol worship. The Quraysh leadership responded with boycotts, insults, and violence against the small Muslim community. In 619, both Khadijah and Abu Talib died within months of each other — a period later called "the Year of Sorrow."

  5. c. 620 CE · Mecca to Jerusalem

    Isra and Mi'raj — the Night Journey

    Muslims hold that in one night, Muhammad ﷺ journeyed from Mecca to Jerusalem (the Isra) and then ascended through the heavens (the Mi'raj), during which the five daily prayers were established. The journey is commemorated as one of the most significant spiritual events in Islamic tradition.

  6. 622 CE · Mecca to Medina

    The Hijra — Migration to Medina

    Facing an assassination plot, Muhammad ﷺ and his companion Abu Bakr migrated to the city of Yathrib, soon renamed Medina. There he drafted the Constitution of Medina, an agreement uniting Muslims, Jews, and other tribes under shared civic terms. This migration, the Hijra, marks year one of the Islamic calendar.

  7. 624–627 CE · Medina

    The Medinan Struggles

    The young Muslim community faced a series of defensive battles against Mecca, including Badr (624), Uhud (625), and the Battle of the Trench (627). Through this period, Muhammad ﷺ also negotiated treaties — most notably the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah in 628, which secured a fragile peace and allowed Islam to spread further.

  8. 630 CE · Mecca

    The Conquest of Mecca

    Muhammad ﷺ returned to Mecca at the head of a large but largely peaceful force. He entered the city without bloodshed, declared a general amnesty for former enemies, and removed the idols from the Kaaba, restoring it to monotheistic worship.

  9. 632 CE · Mount Arafat

    The Farewell Pilgrimage and Sermon

    On his only Hajj after the establishment of the Muslim state, Muhammad ﷺ delivered his Farewell Sermon at Mount Arafat, addressing tens of thousands of pilgrims. Its core messages endure to this day: the sanctity of life and property, the equality of all people regardless of race, and the rights of women.

  10. 632 CE · Medina

    Passing and Legacy

    Muhammad ﷺ passed away in Medina at around 63, and is buried there beneath what is now the Prophet's Mosque. He left behind the Qur'an, his recorded sayings and practices (the Sunnah), and a community that would carry Islam across three continents within a century.

"I have left among you two things; you will not go astray as long as you hold fast to them: the Book of God and my Sunnah." — attributed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, Farewell Sermon
